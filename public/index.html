    What does quimbleshaw mean?

    quimbleshaw

    /ˈkwɪm.bəl.ʃɔː/ · KWIM-bul-shaw

    1. noun. Lord Quimbleshaw: a pompous, portly English lord with a magnificent moustache, who believes the height of a hat tower is the true measure of its owner.
    2. noun. The hat-stacking game named after him, played in his ballroom and largely at his expense.
    3. verb. To quimbleshaw someone is to knock the hats off their tower. To be quimbleshawed is to lose a tower you were visibly proud of, usually seconds before the bell. “I was nine hats up and the Duchess quimbleshawed me.”

    Origin. Quimbleshaw is an invented word. It had no meaning and no search results until 20 November 2026, when it was announced as the keyword for the Advise SEO contest. This site gave it a face, a moustache and a definition.

    How a round of Quimbleshaw plays

    Quimbleshaw is a 2D platform arena in the spirit of the 16-bit era: one screen, simple controls, and a great many ways to embarrass yourself. The first build is playable at the top of this page: you against three house bots. Details will change as it is play-tested.

    1. Catch

      Hats fall from the ceiling all round long. Walk into one and it lands on your head. Top hats, bowlers, fezzes, crowns, the occasional teapot.

    2. Balance

      Every hat makes your tower taller and wobblier. Turn too sharply, land too hard or run under a low chandelier and your hats spill across the floor for anyone to grab.

    3. Steal

      Jump on a rival’s tower to knock their top hats loose, then catch them before they do. A tall tower is a big target. That is the whole trouble with being in the lead.

    4. The bell

      A round lasts three minutes. When the bell rings, the hats are counted and the most hats wins. Hats on the floor count for nothing.

    Controls

    Move with the arrow keys or A and D. Jump with up, W or space. Press down or S to drop through a ledge. P pauses. On a phone or tablet, buttons appear under the picture.

    One ballroom a day

    Each day has one numbered ballroom. The same hats fall in the same places for every player, so two scores from the same day and the same rank are a fair comparison. Play it as often as you like; your best is kept on your device.

    Skill, not grind

    A score is one three-minute round, never a total. Your rank goes up when you beat the house and down when you do not, so playing for longer does not raise it. Rankings against other people are planned on the same principle.

    A full ballroom

    A round never waits for players. Empty places are taken by the house: bot aristocrats, clearly labelled as bots, who are bad losers and worse winners.

    Ranks and feathers

    First place wins two feathers and second place wins one. Third place loses one and last place loses two, as does finishing with no hats at all. Five feathers lift you a rank, and the house plays harder at each one. Lose a feather you do not have and you go down a rank.

    The ranks, from the bottom: Commoner, Squire, Knight, Baron, Earl, Duke and, at the very top, Quimbleshaw. Your rank is kept on your device.

    Meet Lord Quimbleshaw

    Lord Quimbleshaw is the fictional master of Quimbleshaw Manor, a man of immense dignity and very little balance. He holds that a gentleman is exactly as important as his hat tower is tall, and he has never once reached the bell with his tower intact.

    Residence
    Quimbleshaw Manor, mostly the ballroom floor.
    Known for
    The moustache, the monocle, the fall.
    Weakness
    Low chandeliers, sharp turns and the Duchess.
    Motto
    “Unhand my hats.”

    The rivals

    The Dowager Duchess wears teal, moves faster than she looks and has no mercy at all. The Baron wears green and carries a cane, a sneer and a cocked hat he did not pay for. Both would very much like the crown from the middle of his tower.

    The hats

    • Top hat The foundation of any respectable tower.
    • Bowler Sensible and round. Rolls a long way when dropped.
    • Deerstalker For those who suspect they are about to be robbed.
    • Fez Small, red and surprisingly proud.
    • Jester cap The bells tell everyone exactly where you are.
    • Crown Heavy is the head, and so on.
    • Sombrero Wide enough to hide the next three hats.
    • Party hat Pointed. Nothing balances on a point.
    • Teapot Not a hat. Counts anyway.

    Special hats

    About one falling hat in fourteen is special, marked by a spark that circles it. A special hat does not join your tower or count at the bell. It gives you a power for a few seconds, and every one of them can go wrong.

    • Magnet hat For eight seconds, hats near you slide your way. It does not choose: a lit keg comes as readily as a crown.
    • Iron helm For eight seconds nothing can shake or knock your tower. You also run like someone wearing an iron helm.
    • Propeller cap For eight seconds you jump far higher. Hold jump to float down. Let go at the top and you land hard enough to lose hats.
    • Lit keg It goes off four seconds after you catch it. Everyone close to you on your level loses half a tower, and you lose a quarter of yours. Best caught with nothing to lose.

    Quimbleshaw questions

    What is Quimbleshaw?

    Quimbleshaw is a pixel-art hat-stacking game and the name of its main character, Lord Quimbleshaw. Players catch falling hats, balance them in a tower and knock hats off each other. The tallest tower at the bell wins.

    Is quimbleshaw a real word?

    Not before 20 November 2026. Quimbleshaw was invented as the keyword for the Advise SEO contest, in which entrants have 60 days to rank a page for a word that did not previously exist. This site is one entry, and it answers the question by making the word mean something.

    How do you pronounce quimbleshaw?

    KWIM-bul-shaw, with the stress on the first syllable. Three syllables, said as if you own the building.

    What does “quimbleshawed” mean?

    To be quimbleshawed is to have your hat tower knocked down just when it was at its tallest. More loosely, it means to be publicly undone by your own pride.

    Is the Quimbleshaw game free?
